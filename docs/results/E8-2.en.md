# E8-2 Freshness and distribution distance

*日本語版: [E8-2.md](E8-2.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: JS divergence captures the shift in the production distribution, and MMD captures co-adaptive rephrasing
- Planted condition: ProdStream's day-by-day shift (new-category share 0% → 40%) plus rephrasing
- Control: A resample from the same distribution
- Pass criteria: js_monotonic == 1, new_category_alarm == 1, mmd_p_value < 0.05, control_p_ge_005_rate >= 0.9
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
`ProdStream` (the production stand-in of ADR-006) generated 60 sessions for each of days 0–4.
As the days advance, the share of the new category (expense) rises and prompts drift toward
"how a familiar user would say it".
The suite-side distribution is the `category` distribution in the task YAMLs. JS divergence
uses the formula in report 8.3 (base 2).
The alarm threshold is the 95th percentile of JS when resampling the same number from the suite
distribution (bootstrap, simulated).
Co-adaptation is detected by the MMD between the rephrased prompts and the original prompts
(TF-IDF over character 2–3-grams + RBF), with p values from a permutation test. The control is
two groups resampled from the same distribution, where we look at how often p ≥ 0.05.

## Findings and limitations
- JS per day: [0.013826, 0.027569, 0.160572, 0.183587, 0.266444]; new-category share: [0.0, 0.05, 0.267, 0.3, 0.417]
- MMD of the rephrasing: 4.2e-05（p=0.0166）; control p values: [0.6238, 0.4059, 0.4851, 0.2772, 0.604]...
- Production is stood in for by a generator (ADR-006). Both the magnitude of the distribution shift and the manner of rephrasing are ours to choose, so what this experiment shows is that JS and MMD react to a shift — not that they can capture a real operational distribution shift.
- The rephrasing is produced by deterministic substitution rules (`PARAPHRASE_RULES`). The live path, which would have Haiku generate them, is designed but not executed.
