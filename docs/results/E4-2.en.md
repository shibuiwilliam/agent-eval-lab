# E4-2 Detecting wasted calls by ablation

*日本語版: [E4-2.md](E4-2.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: v04's wasted-call rate U is higher than v01's, and the judge surrogate metric agrees well enough with ablation
- Planted condition: 5 runs of v04 and 5 of v01 (every call ablated)
- Control: A call whose result the immediately following action uses (search → create) is judged useful
- Pass criteria: waste_rate_diff >= 0.15, surrogate_precision >= 0.7, surrogate_recall >= 0.7, control_useful_rate >= 0.9
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
Taking 5 runs each of v01_baseline and v04_loopy, we built for every tool call a conversation
in which that call's result is replaced by `[result omitted by ablation]`, restored the
snapshot after step i, and re-executed from step i+1 onwards (report 4.3). If `outcome.passed`
does not change, that call is judged "wasted". The wasted-call rate `U(τ)` is the fraction of
calls judged wasted.
The surrogate metric asks the judge, via `submit_verdict`, whether the result was referenced by
a later decision; precision and recall are computed against the ablation result as ground
truth.
The control is calls whose immediately following action depends on the result, such as
`calendar_search → calendar_create`, where we look at the fraction judged useful.
Because live was unavailable, the ablation re-execution ran in sim mode and the judge is the
deterministic surrogate (`offline_ablation_judge` in `judge/rubric.py`). Provenance is
simulated.

## Findings and limitations
- Ablated call counts: {'v01_baseline': 18, 'v04_loopy': 23}
- The call at the final step cannot have its influence measured, because no decision follows. The implementation counts it on the "wasted" side (the non-conservative side, so U may be overstated).
- The judge is the deterministic surrogate, not live Sonnet. The surrogate metric's precision and recall are the performance of that surrogate, not of an LLM judge. Re-measurement under live is needed.
- The verdict is FAIL (a flaw in the experiment design). U pinned to the ceiling for both versions. The cause is that this environment's agent has a checks_run → repair loop: even with a search result hidden, it detects the duplicate and rebuilds, so removing a result does not change the outcome. Since U measures "would it still pass without this call", it always reads high for an agent with a repair loop. Ablation itself works as specified (the control useful-rate is 1.0); the absence of a gap between versions is a ceiling effect.
