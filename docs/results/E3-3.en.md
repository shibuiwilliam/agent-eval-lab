# E3-3 Uncertainty-driven test selection

*日本語版: [E3-3.md](E3-3.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: At a 30% budget, selection by H(p̂)/c has a lower escape-defect rate than random selection and most-recently-failed, and expected_escape is calibrated against the measured value
- Planted condition: Each change v02–v09 evaluated leave-one-change-out
- Control: Random selection at the same budget (averaged over 1,000 draws) and most-recently-failed
- Pass criteria: escape_ratio_vs_random <= 0.5, calibration_error <= 0.15
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
There are 8 change events, v01 -> v02 through v09. For each event we estimated `p_hat_t` from **the history of the current deployment, v01_baseline** (ADR-022), and greedily selected in descending order of `H(p_hat)/c` up to a 30% budget (redundancy: skip candidates whose normalised edit-distance similarity over the tool-name sequence exceeds 0.8).

**The escape-defect rate is measured two ways.**
1. `Escape(S) = |F_full \ S| / |F_full|` (the definition in report 3.8), where `F_full` is the set of tasks that **failed in that full run** (ADR-018). One full run = each test once, so we build `F_full(r)` per repeat and average over the 10 repeats
2. Flip-based (the pre-revision definition), where `F_full` is the set of tasks whose outcome flipped under the change

Before the revision we used 2 while comparing against `expected_escape`, which predicts the failure mass `sum(1 - p_hat)`. **That subtracted two incompatible quantities**, so the calibration error could not mean anything either way.

The controls are random selection (300 draws per event, averaged) and most-recently-failed; both now include the inviolable set (ADR-020). To isolate which component carries the signal, 5 selection policies are compared on both KPIs. The attainable floor within budget comes from an oracle that knows each repeat's `F_full` in advance.

- **Calibration held.** Comparing `expected_escape` against the measured escape-defect rate on the same quantity (failure-based) gives an error of 0.08 (criterion 0.15). The pre-revision 0.325 was an artefact of subtracting a prediction about failures from a measurement about flips (ADR-018). The selector correctly estimates how many failures it is leaving behind.
- **What did not hold is the selection itself.** The uncertainty-driven escape-defect rate is 0.778 against random's 0.737 — it is **worse** (ratio 1.056, criterion 0.5). Measured on the flip-based KPI the ratio is 1.137, again worse than random. The attainable floor within budget (an oracle that knows each repeat's F_full) is 0.155, so the criterion itself is not unattainable.
- The ablation separates what carries signal from what does not. (a) Selecting on **coverage overlap alone** is the best on the flip KPI at 0.344x random. (b) **Removing the redundancy penalty** improves it to 0.622x. (c) **`H(p_hat)/c` alone** is 0.973x — essentially random. So in this environment the signal lives in report 3.2 (coverage), not in 3.4's uncertainty, and the redundancy penalty actively destroys signal.
- The redundancy penalty drops an average of 31.8 candidates per draw. Per ADR-014 this is not a problem with the similarity definition but with **sim trajectories collapsing to nearly identical tool-name sequences across tasks**. In live trajectories, 10 tasks split into 9 distinct sequences and only 1 of 45 pairs (2%) exceeds a similarity of 0.8.
- The flip definition dropped its threshold in ADR-016 (any discordant paired repeat counts as a flip). Under the pre-revision flake band, v09 showed 4 flips instead of 23 and v06 showed 6 instead of 11 — most real flips were being missed.
- The verdict is NEGATIVE (the implementation follows the report but the claim did not hold). The pre-revision diagnosis was FAIL (our redundancy implementation was at fault); the ablation shows that **even without redundancy, H(p_hat) alone cannot beat random**, so the cause is corrected to the method side.
- The logistic regression was trained leave-one-out at the granularity of change events. With only 8 events of training data, the average with the Beta posterior dominates.
