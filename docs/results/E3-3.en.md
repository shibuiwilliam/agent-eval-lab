# E3-3 Uncertainty-driven test selection

*日本語版: [E3-3.md](E3-3.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: At a 30% budget, selection by H(p̂)/c has a lower escape-defect rate than random selection and most-recently-failed, and expected_escape is calibrated against the measured value
- Planted condition: Each change v02–v09 evaluated leave-one-change-out
- Control: Random selection at the same budget (averaged over 1,000 draws) and most-recently-failed
- Pass criteria: escape_ratio_vs_random <= 0.5, calibration_error <= 0.15
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
There are 8 change events, v01 → v02 through v09. For each event we estimated `p̂_t` from the
history with that event removed (leave-one-change-out) and greedily selected in descending
order of `H(p̂)/c` up to a 30% budget (redundancy: skip candidates whose normalised
edit-distance similarity over the tool-name sequence exceeds 0.8). The escape-defect rate is
the fraction of unselected tests that actually flipped (report 3.8).
The controls are random selection (averaged over 1,000 draws) and most-recently-failed.
`expected_escape` is `Σ(1 − p̂_t) / |T|` over the unselected tests; we placed it next to the
measured escape-defect rate to obtain the calibration error.

- Isolating the cause: the redundancy penalty (from `.claude/rules/pts.md`: "skip candidates whose complement of normalised edit distance over the tool-name sequence exceeds 0.8") drops most of the candidates — an average of 26.8 per draw. In this illustrative environment the tasks' tool-name sequences are nearly identical (e.g. `calendar_search → calendar_create → checks_run → finish`), so a similarity based on tool names alone cannot tell different tasks apart.
- With the redundancy penalty switched off, the escape-defect rate falls to 0.442 (random is 0.704). In other words the path "narrow by coverage → order by uncertainty" does work; what drops the candidates is the similarity definition.
- **Corrected diagnosis (confirmed live, ADR-014)**: this "tasks of the same kind mark each other as duplicates" effect happens because sim trajectories share almost identical tool-name sequences across tasks. In live trajectories, 10 tasks split into 9 distinct sequences and only 1 of 45 pairs (2%) exceeds a similarity of 0.8. The definition of the redundancy penalty is not changed.
- The verdict is FAIL (a flaw in the experiment design). It is not that the method failed to work as claimed; the cause is the combination of an implementation that measures similarity on tool names alone with a task set whose tool-name sequences are homogeneous. Proposed fix: diversify the tasks' tool sequences (ADR-014 withdrew the plan to change the similarity).
- Change events with zero flips have an undefined escape-defect rate and were excluded from the average (the count is recorded in n_measurable_events).
- The logistic regression was trained leave-one-out at the granularity of change events. With only 8 events of training data, the average with the Beta posterior dominates.
