# E3-7 Escape-defect rate, ε-exploration and the inviolable set

*日本語版: [E3-7.md](E3-7.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: At a 40% budget the escape-defect rate is ≤ 0.1. With ε=0.05 a random test is mixed in every time. The inviolable set is always selected regardless of budget
- Planted condition: Comparing full-execution results against selection results across all changes
- Control: A 100% budget → escape-defect rate 0
- Pass criteria: escape_rate <= 0.1, random_included >= 1, inviolable_rate == 1.0, full_budget_escape == 0
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
We selected at budget ratios of 5% / 20% / 40% / 100% and measured the escape-defect rate, the number of random tests mixed in by epsilon-exploration, and the selection rate of the inviolable set (report 3.8).

The escape-defect rate follows report 3.8: `Escape(S) = |F_full \ S| / |F_full|`, where `F_full` is the set of tasks that **failed in that full run** (ADR-018). One full run = each test once, so `F_full(r)` is built per repeat and averaged. The flip-based value (the pre-revision definition) is reported alongside as a diagnostic.

The inviolable set is selected outside the selection logic, so we confirm it is at 100% even at a 5% budget. The attainable floor within budget comes from an oracle that knows each repeat's `F_full` in advance. The history used to estimate uncertainty is restricted to the current deployment, v01_baseline (ADR-022). The control is a 100% budget (run everything -> escape-defect rate 0).

- The inviolable set (T-005, T-011, T-025) is included unconditionally, outside the selection logic. It is selected 100% of the time even at a 5% budget.
- Epsilon-exploration mixes in at least one test regardless of budget. At a 100% budget everything is already selected, so the count reported is the minimum over budgets < 100%.
- The escape-defect rate of <= 0.1 was not met (0.479 at a 40% budget). **The criterion is not unattainable, though**: an oracle that knows each repeat's `F_full` in advance reaches 0.061 at the same budget, so a 40% budget is enough to cover the failures. What falls short is the selection.
- This is consistent with the E3-3 ablation. Removing the redundancy penalty brings it down to 0.383, still far from 0.1. E3-3 compares 5 policies and shows that in this environment the signal lives in coverage overlap (report 3.2), not in H(p_hat) uncertainty (3.4).
- **Corrected diagnosis (confirmed live, ADR-014)**: the "tasks of the same kind mark each other as duplicates" effect happens because sim trajectories collapse to nearly identical tool-name sequences. In live trajectories, 10 tasks split into 9 distinct sequences and only 1 of 45 pairs (2%) exceeds a similarity of 0.8. The similarity definition is unchanged.
- The flip-based escape-defect rate (the pre-revision definition) is 0.318 at a 40% budget. The flip judgement dropped its threshold under ADR-016.
- The verdict is NEGATIVE. **The skeleton of the method holds**: the inviolable set is selected 100% of the time even at a 5% budget, epsilon-exploration mixes in 2 tests every time, and a 100% budget gives an escape-defect rate of 0. What did not hold is only the selection-quality claim, "escape-defect rate <= 0.1 at a 40% budget". The pre-revision diagnosis was FAIL (our redundancy implementation); the E3-3 ablation shows it falls short even without redundancy, so the cause is corrected to the method side.
