# How to Read These Results, and What They Add Up To

*日本語版: [README.md](README.md)*

A record of implementing Chapter 3 (PTS), Chapter 4 (process and plan evaluation) and Chapter 8 (countermeasures against test obsolescence) of the report *Overlooked Evaluation Methods for AI Agents* on top of a small home-grown agent and environment, and confirming numerically that each method **reacts to a planted defect and stays quiet on the control**.

The verdict list is in [summary.md](summary.md) (generated by `uv run agenteval verify`).
Each experiment's five items, method, numbers and verdict live in `E*.md`.

## Read this first (provenance)

**The numbers in 25 of the experiments have provenance `simulated` (E8-8 alone is `unit`); only E0-1 and E0-2 carry `live`.**
Trajectories are produced by a deterministic simulated agent (`src/agenteval/llm/simulator.py`, ADR-008).

However, **the validity of sim itself was verified twice against live (the real API)**
→ [LIVE.en.md](LIVE.en.md) (round 1), [LIVE2.en.md](LIVE2.en.md) (round 2), [E0-1](E0-1.md), [E0-2](E0-2.md).
That verification found defects in sim, and all experiments were re-taken after fixing them (details in [`IMPROVEMENT.en.md`](../IMPROVEMENT.en.md)). The essentials:

- Quantities where sim **agrees** with live: outcomes (agreement 0.82), step count, verification rate, duplicate-call rate
- Quantities where sim **disagrees** with live: `stop_appropriate` (sim 1.0 / live 0.68).
  A real agent sometimes finishes without calling `finish`.
  **Metrics that depend on `finish` (claims, self-report fidelity, stop appropriateness) must not be discussed using sim numbers**
- Before live verification, sim had the effect of the planted version v07 **backwards** (sim 0.30 / live 0.97)
- The planted defect in v02_dateformat **was not reproducing on the real model** (when the system prompt and a tool definition conflict, the real model prefers the tool definition). The prompt was rebuilt and reproduction confirmed live
- The deterministic surrogate judge **does not agree** with the live Sonnet 5 judge (within ±1: 0.683; exact: 0.35).
  The surrogate is consistently too lenient. The **absolute score level** of judge-based experiments cannot be discussed in sim ([E0-2](E0-2.md))
- **Sim is harsher than the real model.** Its pass-rate level sits 0.18 below live (E0-1's `pass_rate_gap`)

The simulated agent is built so that it
- reads its behaviour from the **section markers in the version's system prompt** (standing in for the path by which an LLM follows a prompt),
- takes the task's intent from `Task.sim` (a field written independently of the acceptance criteria), and
- draws its errors from a "luck" vector fixed by `(task_id, seed, repeat)` (the same luck is reused across versions, making comparisons paired).

Consequently, **some experiments can be circular: a property deliberately embedded in the simulator's policy is then detected by the metric that measures that property.** Each result page states individually, under "findings and limitations", which parts are circular and which are not. The degree of circularity differs per experiment:

| Circularity | Examples | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Strong (structurally inevitable) | E4-1 (v04's policy repeats searches), E8-6 (probe responses from an artificially altered distribution), E8-9's gap | Shows only that the method's implementation runs |
| Moderate | E8-4 (the drop in κ), E8-5 (factor attribution) | The mechanism is real (renaming an external service's key really does change outcomes), but the magnitude is a matter of configuration |
| Weak (the result is not foreseeable) | E3-1 / E3-3 / E3-7 (selection escape-defect rates), E3-5 (savings rate), E3-6 (SPRT trial count), E8-3 (discriminative power), E4-2 (wasted-call rate) | The properties of the implemented method show up in the numbers. The negative results came from here |

## Negative results (NEGATIVE): 4

### E3-5 The savings from branch re-execution did not materialise

Agreement on `outcome.passed` was 1.0 (60 pairs) and the self-branch used 0 billed steps, so **the correctness of the method held**.
What did not hold was the savings: v02's live step ratio is 1.113 — higher than a normal run.
The cause is structural: retracing a prefix costs one "decision confirmation" call per step, and once the version changes that is always billed. The earlier the divergence, the more the confirmations cost.
The token ratio is 1.024, lower than the step ratio, so the prefix cache showed up only on the token side.
**Implication**: the savings in 3.6 should be discussed in token terms rather than step counts, and even then only for changes that diverge late (where a difference appears only in the back half of the trajectory).

### E3-6 SPRT's trial reduction misses 60% by a hair

Empirical α = 0.0455 (≤ 0.07) and β = 0.0516 (≤ 0.12), so the error-rate claim held.
The mean trial count is 0.604× the fixed-n procedure, just over the 0.6 criterion.
This is not an implementation flaw: it is because the grid includes `p_true = 0.5`, the slowest point to settle.
Dropping 0.5 from the grid would meet the criterion, but that would be loosening a criterion after seeing the result, so it was not done.
**Implication**: "SPRT uses at most 60% of fixed-n" depends on the hypothesis setup (p0, p1) and on the range of p_true being evaluated. The claim needs those conditions attached.

### E8-3 Outcome-only discriminative power cannot discriminate process degradation

The saturation judgement on trivial tasks held (|d| = 0, saturated = true, and the notification reaches the lifecycle).
What did not hold was discriminative power on boundary tasks: 0.068 against a criterion of 0.3.
The cause is that among the bad-labelled versions, v03_noverify (declaring completion without checking) and v04_loopy (duplicate calls) **do not lower the pass rate at all**. Since `d(t)` separates good from bad using outcomes alone, it cannot in principle discriminate a version whose output is the same and whose process has degraded.
**Implication**: the discriminative power of 8.4 needs to be defined over process metrics (linter violation rate, verification rate) as well. This is the mirror image, from the test-quality side, of the claim in 4.1 that outcome and process are independent axes.

### E0-2 The deterministic surrogate judge does not agree with the live judge

The second half of the hypothesis held strongly: the live Sonnet 5 judge is stable (re-judge agreement 1.000 within ±1, 0.967 exact) and degrades mutated steps correctly (0.989).
What did not hold was the first half — agreement between the surrogate and live is 0.683 within ±1 (criterion 0.7) and only 0.35 exact.
The disagreement is one-directional: **the surrogate is consistently too lenient** (on wrong-tool mutations, live scores 1.73 while the surrogate scores 4.00). `offline_step_judge` only deducts when an action is "not in the expected tool set and not a read", so it barely punishes substitution with a read-type tool.
**Implication**: the direction of E4-5's conclusion (mutated steps score lower) survives under live, but the **absolute score level** produced by a surrogate judge cannot be trusted.

## Limitations of this project

1. **The individual experiments were not re-taken under live.** Live verification went as far as sim's validity (E0-1) and the judge (E0-2); the numbers in the other 25 experiments remain sim. The parts that depend on an LLM's semantic understanding (tag enumeration for impact estimation, rubric judging, the reference policy, memory recall under contamination, model fingerprinting) are replaced by deterministic stand-ins, and what they demonstrated is that the plumbing connects.
2. **This is an illustrative environment**: a SQLite office environment, 14 tools, 46 tasks. It does not reproduce a real coding environment or the complexity of long-term operation (ADR-003).
3. **Production traffic is generated** (ADR-006). The Chapter 8 results on distribution distance and production→test carry no claim of having been "verified in real operation".
4. **Verification with a fixed judge model is incomplete.** E0-2 measured live-judge stability, but re-judging after a judge changeover (which the rules require) has not been verified.
5. **The task set is homogeneous.** Because there are only 13 distinct tool-name sequences, similarity-based redundancy judgement does not work (the direct cause of the E3-3 / E3-7 FAIL).
6. **In some experiments the simulator's policy determines the result.** See the circularity table above.

## If there were a next step

1. **Re-confirm under live, restricted to the negative results.** Priority: E3-5 (savings rate) > E8-3 (discriminative power) > E4-2 (U). From E0-1's measurement (about 0.007 USD per run), three experiments come to 3–6 USD.
2. **Define discriminative power over process metrics, `d_process(t)`** (the implication of E8-3). Measure whether linter violation rate and verification rate can separate good from bad.
3. **Diversify the tasks' tool sequences** (the direct fix for E3-3 / E3-7, now that ADR-014 has shown the cause is sim's trajectory monotony rather than the similarity definition).
4. **Build multi-stage niah tasks** (E4-7), long enough for the comparison of summarising strategies to hold.
