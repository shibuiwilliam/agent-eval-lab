# E8-7 Production→test pipeline

*日本語版: [E8-7.md](E8-7.md)*

## The five items
- Hypothesis: The stratified sampler prioritises important sessions, the drafted acceptance criteria are approved, and registering them shrinks the distance between the suite and production
- Planted condition: 20 sessions drawn from 3 days of ProdStream sessions
- Control: Uniform sampling
- Pass criteria: stratified_lift >= 3.0, approval_rate >= 0.7, js_after_lt_before == 1
- Provenance: simulated

## Method
`ProdStream` generated 3 days of sessions (20 each); those corresponding to existing tasks were
executed as those tasks under v01_baseline to build a body of "production traffic" runs
(ADR-006).
The stratified sampler treats failed runs, high-cost runs (above the per-category p95) and new
categories as strata and draws an equal number from each; the control is uniform sampling. Lift
is "the fraction of important sessions (failed / high-cost / new-category) captured by
stratification ÷ the same fraction under uniform sampling".
From the sampled runs we had the judge draft the equivalent of `draft_task` (a deterministic
drafter, since live was unavailable) and computed the approval rate using "the acceptance
criteria are expressible with known check functions alone" as the approval condition
(equivalent to `--auto-approve`; the result page says "auto-approved" explicitly).
We compared the JS divergence between the suite's category distribution and production's before
and after registration.

## Findings and limitations
- Approval is automatic (equivalent to `--auto-approve`). The approval condition is that the acceptance criteria are expressible with known check functions alone.
- Sessions in the new category (expense) have no definition in `tasks/`, so an ad-hoc Task is assembled and executed (`prodstream.new_category_task`). That is the 8.2 path of "taking in what production has and the suite does not".
- Category breakdown of the drafted tests: {'scheduling': 3, 'expense': 6, 'files': 2, 'mixed': 3, 'mail': 6}
- Production is stood in for by a generator (ADR-006). Provenance is simulated (synthetic prod). We make no claim to have "drafted tests from real operational sessions".
- The verdict is FAIL (a flaw in the experiment design). Stratification did raise the capture rate for important sessions from 0.45 to 0.85, but lift came to 1.889 against a criterion of 3.0. **This criterion is unattainable in principle for this production stream**: uniform sampling already captures 45% important sessions (failed / high-cost / new-category), so lift is capped at 1 / 0.45 = 2.2. The draft approval rate (1.0) and the shrinkage of JS from 0.100 to 0.018 after registration did hold. Proposed fix: lower the production stream's failure rate (making important sessions rare), or write the criterion as an absolute capture rate rather than a lift (needs an ADR).
